Acute Bronchitis

Understanding acute bronchitis, its causes, symptoms, treatment approaches, home care, prevention, and medicine safety.

What is acute bronchitis?

Acute bronchitis, sometimes called a chest cold, occurs when the airways in the lungs become inflamed and produce mucus.

The inflammation and mucus can cause coughing, which may occur with or without mucus production.

Acute bronchitis is usually caused by a virus and commonly improves on its own. Symptoms generally last less than three weeks.

Common symptoms

Symptoms may include:

  • Cough with or without mucus
  • Chest discomfort or soreness when coughing
  • Sore throat
  • Nasal congestion
  • Tiredness
  • Mild body aches
  • Mild headache
  • Mild fever
  • Shortness of breath may occur in some people

Causes

Viral infections

Viruses are the most common cause of acute bronchitis. The same types of respiratory viruses that cause colds and influenza can cause bronchitis.

Bacterial infections

Bacteria can sometimes be associated with bronchitis, but most uncomplicated acute bronchitis does not require antibiotic treatment.

Smoke and irritants

Cigarette smoke, secondhand smoke, dust, fumes, and air pollution can irritate the airways and worsen respiratory symptoms.

Important:

The color of mucus alone does not prove that a respiratory infection is bacterial.

Acute vs chronic bronchitis

Acute bronchitis

Acute bronchitis is usually short-term and often follows a respiratory infection. Symptoms commonly improve within a few weeks.

Chronic bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis is a long-term condition and is associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). It requires a different clinical evaluation and management approach.

Remember:

Persistent or repeated episodes of cough should be medically assessed rather than automatically assumed to be acute bronchitis.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually based on the person's symptoms and clinical examination.

An important part of assessment is determining whether another condition, such as pneumonia, asthma, or another respiratory disorder, could explain the symptoms.

Additional tests may be needed when symptoms are severe, unusual, persistent, or when another diagnosis is suspected.

Treatment

Most uncomplicated acute bronchitis improves without specific treatment. Management usually focuses on relieving symptoms and supporting recovery.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics are not routinely recommended for uncomplicated acute bronchitis because most cases are caused by viruses.

Antibiotics do not treat viral infections and unnecessary use can cause adverse effects and contribute to antimicrobial resistance.

Symptom relief

Depending on age, medical history, and other medicines being used, a healthcare professional or pharmacist may recommend suitable medicines to help relieve symptoms.

When another infection is suspected

Conditions such as pneumonia or pertussis can produce symptoms similar to bronchitis. If another condition is identified, treatment should be based on the appropriate diagnosis.

Antibiotic safety:

Do not start antibiotics simply because a cough produces mucus. Antibiotic treatment should be based on appropriate clinical assessment.

Home care

Most people with uncomplicated acute bronchitis can support recovery with simple measures.

Get enough rest

Adequate rest can help the body recover from respiratory infection.

Drink enough fluids

Drink adequate fluids to help maintain hydration.

Warm drinks

Warm drinks may help soothe an irritated throat and provide comfort.

Honey for cough

Honey may help relieve cough in adults and children aged one year or older.

Humidified air

A clean humidifier or cool-mist vaporizer may help make the air more comfortable when the respiratory tract is irritated.

Avoid smoke

Avoid cigarette smoke, secondhand smoke, dust, and other respiratory irritants.

Important:

Honey should not be given to infants younger than one year because of the risk of infant botulism.

Medicine safety

  • Do not use antibiotics unless they have been appropriately prescribed or recommended.
  • Read the active ingredients of over-the-counter cough and cold medicines.
  • Avoid taking several products containing the same active ingredient.
  • Ask a healthcare professional about medicines that are appropriate for children.
  • Tell a healthcare professional about all prescription and non-prescription medicines being used.
  • People with chronic heart, lung, kidney, or other medical conditions should seek individualized advice when symptoms develop.

Prevention

Because many cases of acute bronchitis are caused by respiratory viruses, preventing respiratory infections can reduce the risk.

  • Wash hands regularly with soap and water.
  • Cover the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are sick when possible.
  • Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke.
  • Improve indoor air quality when possible.
  • Keep recommended vaccinations up to date.

When to seek medical care

Medical assessment is important when symptoms are severe, persistent, or concerning.

  • Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  • Coughing up blood or bloody mucus
  • Symptoms lasting more than three weeks
  • Repeated episodes of bronchitis
  • High or persistent fever
  • Significant chest pain
  • Symptoms that are rapidly worsening
  • Feeling severely unwell
Emergency:

Severe difficulty breathing, confusion, blue or very pale lips/skin, or inability to speak because of breathing difficulty requires urgent medical attention.

Educational information

This page provides general health education about acute bronchitis. It does not replace professional examination, diagnosis, or individualized treatment.

If symptoms are severe, persistent, unusual, or worsening, seek assessment from a qualified healthcare professional.

Seek medical care

Difficulty breathing, coughing up blood, persistent symptoms, or severe worsening requires medical assessment.
