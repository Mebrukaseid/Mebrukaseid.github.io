UPPER RESPIRATORY CONDITION

Sinusitis

Understanding sinusitis, its symptoms, causes, treatment, home care, prevention, and medicine safety.

What is sinusitis?

Sinusitis is inflammation of the tissues lining the sinuses. It can occur during respiratory infections and may be caused by viruses, bacteria, or other factors.

Sinusitis may be short-term (acute) or persist for a longer period (chronic).

Common symptoms

Sinusitis may cause:

  • Blocked or stuffy nose
  • Nasal discharge
  • Facial pressure or pain
  • Headache
  • Reduced sense of smell
  • Cough
  • Sore throat from postnasal drainage

Causes and risk factors

Sinusitis can develop after a respiratory infection, particularly a viral infection.

Other factors may contribute to sinus problems, including allergies and conditions that interfere with normal drainage from the sinuses.

Acute and chronic sinusitis

Acute sinusitis

Acute sinusitis usually develops over a relatively short period and commonly occurs after an upper respiratory infection.

Chronic sinusitis

Chronic sinusitis involves persistent or recurrent sinus symptoms over a longer period and may require further evaluation to identify contributing factors.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and severity of the condition. Many cases of acute sinusitis improve without antibiotics.

Supportive treatment

Supportive measures may include adequate fluids, rest, and measures that help relieve nasal congestion and discomfort.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics are not useful for viral infections. They may be considered when bacterial sinusitis is suspected after appropriate clinical assessment.

Other treatment

Depending on the underlying cause, healthcare professionals may recommend treatments directed at nasal inflammation or allergies.

Important:

Do not start antibiotics simply because facial pressure, nasal discharge, or congestion is present. The cause and clinical course should be considered.

Home care

For mild symptoms, supportive care may help improve comfort while the condition resolves.

Rest

Get adequate rest while recovering from respiratory illness.

Fluids

Drink adequate fluids to help maintain hydration.

Warm fluids

Warm drinks may provide comfort when there is throat irritation or discomfort.

Saline nasal care

Saline nasal preparations may help moisturize the nasal passages and clear mucus.

Safety:

If nasal irrigation is used, appropriate safe water and proper cleaning practices are important. Do not use unsafe water directly in the nose.

Medicine safety

Some medicines used for nasal congestion may not be appropriate for everyone. People with conditions such as hypertension, heart disease, glaucoma, or other medical conditions should seek professional advice before using certain decongestants.

Multiple cold and sinus products may also contain the same active ingredients. Always check the ingredients before combining medicines.

Prevention

  • Wash hands regularly.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are sick when possible.
  • Avoid cigarette smoke and other respiratory irritants.
  • Manage known allergies appropriately.
  • Maintain good general respiratory hygiene.

When to seek medical care

Medical assessment is important when symptoms are severe, persistent, or associated with concerning features.

  • Severe headache or facial pain
  • Swelling around the eyes
  • Vision changes
  • Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms
  • Symptoms that persist or repeatedly return

Educational information

This page provides general health education and does not replace diagnosis, prescribing, or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional.

Important

Severe facial pain, swelling around the eyes, vision changes, or rapidly worsening symptoms require medical assessment.

Home care

Rest, adequate fluids, and appropriate supportive care can help with mild symptoms.
