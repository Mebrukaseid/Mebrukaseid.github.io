MEDICINES

Pain Medicines

Understanding common pain medicines, how they work, important side effects, interactions, and safe use.

What Are Pain Medicines?

Pain medicines, also called analgesics, are medicines used to reduce pain. Different medicines are appropriate for different types and causes of pain.

Pain may result from injury, inflammation, infection, surgery, nerve damage, chronic disease, or many other conditions. Treating the underlying cause is often an important part of pain management.

Important:

The safest pain medicine depends on the type of pain, age, other diseases, kidney and liver function, pregnancy status, and other medicines.

Major Types of Pain Medicines

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

Paracetamol is commonly used for mild to moderate pain and fever. It has little anti-inflammatory activity compared with NSAIDs.

NSAIDs

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduce pain and inflammation. Examples include ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, and aspirin when used for appropriate indications.

Opioids

Opioids such as morphine, oxycodone, and fentanyl can be used for certain moderate to severe pain. They require careful clinical supervision because they can cause respiratory depression, sedation, constipation, tolerance, dependence, and overdose.

Medicines for Neuropathic Pain

Some pain caused by nerve damage responds better to medicines that are not traditional analgesics. Examples include certain antidepressants and anticonvulsants such as amitriptyline, duloxetine, and gabapentin.

Topical Pain Medicines

Some pain can be treated with medicines applied directly to the skin or affected area. Examples include topical NSAIDs and local anesthetic preparations.

How Do Pain Medicines Work?

Pain medicines work through different mechanisms.

  • Paracetamol mainly acts through central mechanisms involved in pain and temperature regulation.
  • NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase enzymes and reduce prostaglandin production.
  • Opioids activate opioid receptors in the central and peripheral nervous systems.
  • Some medicines for neuropathic pain reduce abnormal nerve signaling.
  • Local anesthetics temporarily block nerve conduction.

Paracetamol Safety

Paracetamol is widely used, but taking too much can cause serious liver injury.

  • Check whether other medicines also contain paracetamol.
  • Avoid accidentally combining multiple paracetamol-containing products.
  • People with significant liver disease or other risk factors should seek professional advice.
  • Follow the product label or prescribed instructions.
Overdose warning:

Suspected paracetamol overdose requires urgent medical assessment, even if the person initially feels well.

NSAIDs

NSAIDs can reduce pain, inflammation, and fever, but they can also cause important adverse effects.

Important Risks

  • Stomach irritation and ulcers
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Kidney injury
  • Fluid retention
  • Increased blood pressure in some people
  • Cardiovascular risks in susceptible patients
  • Allergic or asthma-related reactions in susceptible individuals

Who Should Be Careful With NSAIDs?

NSAIDs may not be appropriate for everyone. Extra caution is important in people with:

  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Heart failure
  • Uncontrolled hypertension
  • Previous stomach ulcer or gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Certain types of asthma or NSAID hypersensitivity
  • Pregnancy, particularly later pregnancy

Opioid Medicines

Opioids can be highly effective for certain types of severe pain, but they require careful assessment and monitoring.

Important Risks

  • Drowsiness and sedation
  • Constipation
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Confusion
  • Tolerance and physical dependence
  • Respiratory depression
  • Overdose
Safety:

Opioids should only be used according to appropriate clinical instructions. Combining opioids with alcohol or other sedating medicines can dangerously increase respiratory depression.

Important Interactions

NSAIDs + Anticoagulants

Combining NSAIDs with anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines can increase bleeding risk.

NSAIDs + ACE Inhibitors / ARBs + Diuretics

This combination can increase the risk of kidney problems in susceptible patients and may affect blood pressure control.

Opioids + Sedatives

Combining opioids with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other central nervous system depressants can increase sedation and respiratory depression.

Multiple Paracetamol Products

Taking several products that contain paracetamol can unintentionally result in excessive total exposure.

Pain Medicines & Kidney Function

Kidney function is an important consideration when selecting and monitoring pain medicines.

NSAIDs can reduce kidney blood flow and may worsen kidney function in susceptible patients. Some other analgesics also require individualized adjustment when kidney function is reduced.

Important:

People with chronic kidney disease should ask a healthcare professional before regularly using NSAIDs or other pain medicines.

Pain Medicines & Liver Function

Liver function can also affect the safety of analgesics.

Excessive paracetamol exposure can cause serious liver injury. Other medicines may also require special consideration in people with significant liver disease.

Pregnancy & Breastfeeding

Pain treatment during pregnancy and breastfeeding should consider both the mother's needs and the potential effects on the infant.

NSAIDs require particular caution during pregnancy, especially later in pregnancy. The choice of an analgesic should therefore be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

Pain Medicines in Children

Children are not simply small adults. Medicine selection and dosing can depend on age, weight, clinical condition, and formulation.

Caregivers should use children's medicines according to appropriate professional or product instructions and should avoid giving adult medicines without appropriate guidance.

Safe Use of Pain Medicines

  • Identify the cause of pain when possible.
  • Use the lowest effective exposure for the appropriate duration when clinically suitable.
  • Read active ingredients on OTC products.
  • Avoid taking multiple products containing the same active ingredient.
  • Tell healthcare professionals about all medicines, supplements, and herbal products.
  • Avoid alcohol when using medicines that can interact with it.
  • Store medicines safely and keep them away from children.

When Pain Needs Medical Assessment

Pain should not always be treated simply by taking a painkiller. Medical assessment may be important when pain is severe, persistent, unexplained, or associated with other concerning symptoms.

  • Severe or sudden chest pain
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Severe headache with neurological symptoms
  • Severe pain following a major injury
  • Pain associated with difficulty breathing
  • Pain with fainting or severe weakness
  • Persistent or progressively worsening pain

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Safe pain management requires consideration of:

  • Type and cause of pain
  • Severity and duration
  • Patient age
  • Kidney and liver function
  • Cardiovascular and gastrointestinal risk
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • Current medicines and interactions
  • Previous adverse reactions
  • Risk of dependence with certain medicines

Educational Information

This page provides general information about pain medicines and does not replace individualized medical assessment or professional advice.

The appropriate medicine depends on the cause and severity of pain and the individual's medical history, current medicines, and risk factors.

Pain Is a Signal

Pain medicines can reduce symptoms, but persistent or unexplained pain may require assessment of the underlying cause.

Check the Ingredients

Many combination products contain the same active ingredients found in other medicines. Always check the label before combining products.

Patient Safety

Kidney disease, heart failure, gastrointestinal disease, pregnancy, and other conditions can change which pain medicines are appropriate.
