INFECTIOUS DISEASE

Malaria

Understanding malaria, its symptoms, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and medication safety.

What is malaria?

Malaria is a potentially serious infectious disease caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium. It is transmitted to people mainly through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

Common symptoms

  • Fever
  • Chills
  • Headache
  • Muscle aches and weakness
  • Fatigue
  • Nausea or vomiting

Malaria can become severe. Severe symptoms require urgent medical assessment.

How is malaria diagnosed?

Diagnosis is generally based on clinical assessment together with laboratory testing. Blood-based tests such as microscopy or rapid diagnostic tests can be used to detect malaria parasites or parasite antigens.

Treatment

Malaria is treatable, but the appropriate medicine depends on the type of malaria, severity of illness, age or body weight, pregnancy status, and local patterns of antimalarial resistance.

Uncomplicated malaria

For uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria, WHO recommends an artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT). Examples include artemether-lumefantrine, artesunate-amodiaquine, dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine, and other WHO-recommended ACTs.

Severe malaria

Severe malaria is a medical emergency and requires urgent treatment in a health facility. Injectable antimalarial treatment, such as intravenous or intramuscular artesunate, is used for severe disease before appropriate follow-on treatment is provided.

Plasmodium vivax and other species

Treatment differs according to the malaria species. For P. vivax and P. ovale, additional treatment may be needed to prevent relapse. Primaquine may be used for this purpose after appropriate assessment, including consideration of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) status.

Important:

Antimalarial treatment should be selected by a qualified healthcare professional according to current national treatment guidelines. Do not self-select an antimalarial or stop treatment early without professional advice.

Prevention

  • Use insecticide-treated mosquito nets.
  • Reduce exposure to mosquito bites.
  • Follow recommended malaria prevention measures in areas where malaria transmission occurs.
  • Seek medical assessment when malaria symptoms develop.

Medicine safety

Antimalarial medicines should be taken according to the prescribed or recommended regimen. The correct medicine and duration depend on the type and severity of malaria and individual patient factors.

Do not stop treatment early simply because symptoms improve unless a qualified healthcare professional advises a change.

When to seek urgent care

Severe illness, altered consciousness, seizures, difficulty breathing, severe weakness, or other rapidly worsening symptoms require urgent medical attention.
