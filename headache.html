COMMON CONDITION

Understanding Headache.

Headache is a common symptom with many possible causes. Understanding the pattern, triggers, warning signs, and appropriate medicine use can support safer health decisions.

WHAT IS A HEADACHE?

More than just head pain.

A headache is pain or discomfort involving the head or upper neck. Headaches can vary in location, intensity, duration, and associated symptoms.

Most headaches improve without serious complications, but a new, severe, unusual, or rapidly worsening headache may require medical assessment.

COMMON TYPES

Different headaches can feel different.

Tension Headache

Often feels like pressure or tightening around the head. Stress, muscle tension, poor posture, and fatigue may contribute.

Migraine

May cause moderate to severe throbbing pain, sometimes with nausea and increased sensitivity to light or sound.

Cluster Headache

A less common headache disorder involving severe pain, usually on one side of the head around the eye, occurring in repeated attacks.

Secondary Headache

A headache caused by another condition, medicine, injury, infection, or other underlying problem.

COMMON TRIGGERS

What can trigger a headache?

Headaches may have more than one trigger. Identifying patterns can help people understand what may contribute to recurring headaches.

Common contributors

  • Stress and emotional tension
  • Dehydration
  • Skipping or delaying meals
  • Lack of sleep or changes in sleep pattern
  • Poor posture or muscle tension
  • Eye strain or prolonged screen use
  • Colds and flu
  • Alcohol
  • Changes in caffeine intake
  • Frequent use of painkillers
Health awareness: Keeping a headache diary can help identify patterns such as timing, duration, possible triggers, and response to treatment.

SELF-CARE

Simple steps that may help.

Stay Hydrated

Drink enough fluids and avoid becoming dehydrated, especially during illness or hot weather.

Rest Well

Adequate sleep and regular rest can support headache management.

Eat Regularly

Avoid regularly skipping meals, as irregular eating can contribute to headaches in some people.

Reduce Eye Strain

Take breaks from prolonged screen use and pay attention to posture and visual comfort.

Stay Active

Regular physical activity and relaxation strategies may help reduce some recurring headaches.

Track Patterns

Record when headaches occur, how long they last, possible triggers, and associated symptoms.

MEDICINE SAFETY

Pain relief medicines need safe use.

Medicines such as paracetamol and ibuprofen may be used for some headaches, but the appropriate choice depends on the person, symptoms, other medicines, and medical conditions.

Avoid unnecessary or frequent painkiller use.

Using painkillers too frequently can itself contribute to recurring headaches. Follow the product instructions and seek professional advice if headaches regularly require pain medicine.

People with conditions such as kidney disease, stomach ulcers, cardiovascular disease, pregnancy, or those taking other medicines should ask a healthcare professional which painkiller is appropriate.

IMPORTANT WARNING SIGNS

When should a headache not be ignored?

Most headaches are not emergencies, but certain features require urgent medical assessment.

01

Sudden Severe Pain

A headache that begins suddenly and is extremely painful needs urgent medical assessment.

02

Neurological Symptoms

Weakness, numbness, difficulty speaking, confusion, problems with balance, or seizures are warning signs.

03

Vision Problems

New loss of vision or significant visual symptoms together with a severe headache require urgent assessment.

04

Fever or Stiff Neck

A severe headache with high fever, stiff neck, confusion, or marked drowsiness can be an emergency.

05

After Head Injury

A significant headache after a head injury should be medically assessed.

06

Worsening or Recurrent

Headaches that repeatedly occur, become more severe, or do not improve with usual measures should be evaluated.

HEALTH AWARENESS

Understand the symptom. Know when to act.

A headache is common, but the pattern and accompanying symptoms matter. Good health awareness means knowing when simple self-care may be reasonable and when professional assessment is needed.

Important

This page is intended for general health education and awareness. It does not diagnose headache or replace assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

If a headache is sudden, extremely severe, associated with neurological symptoms, significant vision loss, confusion, seizure, high fever with a stiff neck, or follows a serious head injury, seek urgent medical care.
