RESPIRATORY INFECTION

COVID-19

Understanding COVID-19, its symptoms, transmission, treatment approaches, prevention, and safe medicine use.

What is COVID-19?

COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. It mainly affects the respiratory system, although it can affect other organs and body systems.

COVID-19 can range from an infection with no symptoms to mild illness, severe disease, or critical illness. Most people with mild illness recover without hospitalization, while some people have a higher risk of becoming seriously ill.

Common symptoms

Symptoms can vary from person to person. Common symptoms may include:

  • Fever or feeling feverish
  • Cough
  • Sore throat
  • Runny or blocked nose
  • Tiredness or weakness
  • Headache
  • Muscle or body aches
  • Shortness of breath
  • Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea
  • Changes in taste or smell
Important:

Symptoms can overlap with influenza and other respiratory infections. Symptoms alone cannot always determine which infection is present.

How does COVID-19 spread?

SARS-CoV-2 spreads mainly through infectious respiratory particles released when an infected person breathes, talks, coughs, or sneezes.

Transmission is more likely when people are close together, particularly in crowded or poorly ventilated indoor spaces.

Reducing transmission

  • Improve ventilation in indoor spaces.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are sick when possible.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes.
  • Wash hands regularly.
  • Follow current local public health advice when respiratory infections are increasing.

Who is at higher risk of severe illness?

The risk of severe COVID-19 increases with age and with certain underlying health conditions.

  • Older adults, particularly people over 65 years.
  • People with chronic heart disease.
  • People with chronic lung disease.
  • People with diabetes.
  • People with chronic kidney or liver disease.
  • People with obesity or other significant medical conditions.
  • People with weakened immune systems.
  • People receiving immunosuppressive treatment.
  • People with cancer or other conditions that increase the risk of severe infection.

Higher-risk people should seek medical advice early when COVID-19 symptoms develop because some antiviral treatments need to be started early.

Diagnosis and testing

COVID-19 can be diagnosed using tests that detect SARS-CoV-2 infection. Common tests include molecular tests such as PCR or other nucleic acid amplification tests, and antigen tests.

Testing can be particularly important for people at higher risk of severe illness because an early diagnosis may help determine whether antiviral treatment is appropriate.

Remember:

A negative test does not always completely exclude infection, particularly when testing is performed very early or when symptoms strongly suggest infection.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the severity of illness and the person's risk of developing severe disease.

Mild illness

People with mild COVID-19 who do not have risk factors for severe disease can often be managed at home with supportive care and monitoring.

Higher-risk patients

People with mild or moderate COVID-19 who have risk factors for severe disease may be eligible for antiviral treatment. Antiviral medicines are most effective when started early after symptoms begin.

Depending on clinical circumstances and availability, treatment options may include medicines such as nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or remdesivir. Molnupiravir may be considered in selected adults when preferred options cannot be used or are unavailable.

Severe COVID-19

Severe or critical COVID-19 may require hospital treatment. Care can include oxygen therapy, supportive treatment, and other medicines selected according to disease severity and clinical assessment.

Important:

Antiviral medicines require professional assessment. Some have important drug interactions or restrictions related to kidney function, liver function, pregnancy, age, and other medicines.

Home care

People with mild COVID-19 who have been advised to recover at home can focus on symptom management, rest, hydration, and monitoring.

Rest

Get adequate rest and avoid strenuous physical activity while feeling unwell.

Drink enough fluids

Maintain adequate fluid intake to help prevent dehydration, unless a healthcare professional has advised fluid restriction.

Eat nutritious foods

Maintain adequate nutrition during recovery. Small meals may be easier to tolerate when appetite is reduced.

Monitor symptoms

Pay attention to breathing, worsening symptoms, chest discomfort, confusion, severe weakness, or other changes that may require medical attention.

Reduce transmission

Follow current public health advice and avoid close contact with others while infectious, especially with people at high risk of severe disease.

Medicine safety

Safe medicine use is especially important in people taking several medicines or living with chronic diseases.

  • Do not start an antiviral without professional assessment.
  • Tell the healthcare professional about all medicines, supplements, and herbal products being used.
  • Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir can have important drug-drug interactions.
  • Some COVID-19 treatments require consideration of kidney or liver function.
  • Antibiotics do not treat COVID-19 itself because COVID-19 is caused by a virus.
  • Avoid unnecessary self-medication.
Medication review matters:

People taking multiple medicines should have their medicines reviewed by a qualified healthcare professional before starting certain COVID-19 treatments.

Prevention

Prevention focuses on reducing exposure and protecting people who are at higher risk of severe illness.

  • Follow current COVID-19 vaccination recommendations applicable to your situation.
  • Improve indoor ventilation.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are sick.
  • Stay home when experiencing a contagious respiratory infection when possible.
  • Practice good hand and respiratory hygiene.
  • Use appropriate protective measures in high-risk or crowded settings when advised.

When to seek urgent medical care

Seek urgent medical attention if serious symptoms develop, including:

  • Difficulty breathing, especially at rest.
  • Severe or rapidly worsening breathing problems.
  • Persistent chest pain or pressure.
  • New confusion.
  • Severe drowsiness or difficulty waking.
  • Bluish or unusually pale skin or lips.
  • Severe weakness or inability to perform normal activities.
  • Other symptoms that are severe or rapidly worsening.
Do not wait:

Severe breathing difficulty, confusion, persistent chest pressure, or bluish skin or lips requires urgent medical assessment.

After COVID-19

Most people recover from COVID-19, but some people can experience symptoms that persist or appear after the initial infection.

Post-COVID-19 condition can involve symptoms such as fatigue, shortness of breath, and difficulty with concentration or thinking. Symptoms can fluctuate and may affect daily activities.

Persistent or concerning symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional so that other possible causes can also be assessed.

Educational information

This page provides general health education and is not a substitute for diagnosis, prescription, individualized treatment, or professional medical advice.

Treatment recommendations can change as evidence, guidelines, circulating variants, and available medicines change. Always follow current guidance from qualified healthcare professionals and relevant health authorities.

Seek help when needed

Severe breathing difficulty, chest pressure, confusion, or bluish lips or skin can indicate serious illness and requires urgent medical care.
