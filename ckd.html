CHRONIC DISEASE

Chronic Kidney Disease

Understanding chronic kidney disease, its causes, stages, symptoms, treatment approaches, medicines, monitoring, and ways to protect kidney health.

What is Chronic Kidney Disease?

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a long-term condition in which the kidneys are damaged or their function remains reduced over time.

The kidneys help remove waste and excess fluid from the blood and contribute to regulation of blood pressure, electrolytes, acid-base balance, and other important body functions.

Important:

CKD may have few or no symptoms in its early stages. Testing can therefore be important for people at increased risk, particularly people with diabetes, hypertension, or cardiovascular disease.

Common Causes

CKD can develop from many different conditions that damage the kidneys over time.

  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Hypertension
  • Glomerular diseases
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Recurrent or severe kidney injury
  • Some inherited kidney disorders
  • Certain infections
  • Some medicines, toxins, or other harmful exposures

Risk Factors

  • Diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Family history of kidney disease
  • Older age
  • Obesity
  • Smoking
  • Previous kidney disease or kidney injury

Symptoms

Early CKD may cause no obvious symptoms. As kidney function becomes more severely reduced, symptoms may become more noticeable.

  • Tiredness or weakness
  • Swelling of the legs or feet
  • Shortness of breath
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Poor appetite
  • Itching
  • Muscle cramps
  • Changes in urination

Diagnosis & Kidney Monitoring

CKD evaluation commonly involves blood and urine testing together with assessment of the underlying cause and other health conditions.

Blood tests

  • Serum creatinine
  • Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)
  • Electrolytes when appropriate
  • Other tests depending on the suspected cause

Urine tests

  • Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR)
  • Urinalysis when indicated
Remember:

CKD is not diagnosed from a single abnormal result alone in every situation. Persistence over time and the clinical context are important.

Stages of Chronic Kidney Disease

CKD is classified using the cause of kidney disease, estimated glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and albuminuria.

G1 — Normal or high GFR

eGFR ≥90 mL/min/1.73 m². Kidney damage must still be present for CKD to be diagnosed.

G2 — Mildly decreased GFR

eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m², with evidence of kidney damage when classified as CKD.

G3a — Mildly to moderately decreased

eGFR 45–59 mL/min/1.73 m².

G3b — Moderately to severely decreased

eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m².

G4 — Severely decreased

eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 m².

G5 — Kidney failure

eGFR below 15 mL/min/1.73 m². Some people with kidney failure require kidney replacement therapy such as dialysis or transplantation.

Clinical point:

GFR category alone does not describe the complete risk picture. Albuminuria and the underlying cause are also important.

Understanding Albuminuria

Albuminuria means that albumin, a protein normally retained in the blood, is being lost into the urine.

Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) helps assess the amount of albumin in urine and contributes to CKD classification and risk assessment.

A1

ACR less than 30 mg/g.

A2

ACR 30–300 mg/g.

A3

ACR greater than 300 mg/g.

Treatment & Management

CKD treatment depends on its cause, stage, albuminuria, cardiovascular risk, symptoms, complications, and other health conditions.

Main goals

  • Slow progression of kidney disease
  • Reduce cardiovascular risk
  • Control blood pressure
  • Improve diabetes management when applicable
  • Reduce harmful medication exposure
  • Detect and manage CKD complications

Medicines Used in CKD

Medicines are selected according to the cause of CKD, kidney function, albuminuria, cardiovascular risk, diabetes status, and other individual factors.

ACE Inhibitors and ARBs

Medicines such as enalapril, lisinopril, losartan, and valsartan may be used in appropriate patients, particularly when hypertension and albuminuria are present.

SGLT2 Inhibitors

Medicines such as dapagliflozin and empagliflozin may provide kidney and cardiovascular benefits for appropriate people with CKD, including some people with diabetes.

Diuretics

Diuretics may be used when fluid retention, edema, or hypertension requires treatment.

Statins

Lipid-lowering treatment may be appropriate to reduce cardiovascular risk in many adults with CKD.

Other medicines

Additional medicines may be required depending on complications such as anemia, mineral and bone disorders, metabolic abnormalities, or the underlying kidney disease.

Medication Safety in CKD

Reduced kidney function can change how medicines are eliminated from the body. Some medicines may therefore require dose adjustment, additional monitoring, or avoidance.

  • Check kidney function when medication dosing depends on renal function.
  • Avoid unnecessary use of nephrotoxic medicines.
  • NSAIDs can be harmful to kidney function in some patients and should not be used routinely without appropriate advice.
  • Tell healthcare professionals about all prescription medicines, OTC medicines, supplements, and herbal products.
  • Do not change medicine doses based only on a previous kidney test.
  • Kidney function and electrolytes may need monitoring after certain medicine changes.
Clinical pharmacy point:

Medication review becomes particularly important when kidney function changes, several medicines are being used, or the patient develops an acute illness.

Possible Complications

Advanced CKD can affect many body systems.

  • Anemia
  • Electrolyte abnormalities
  • Metabolic acidosis
  • Fluid retention and edema
  • Mineral and bone disorders
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Increased risk of acute kidney injury
  • Kidney failure

Home Care & Kidney Protection

Daily habits and regular medical follow-up can help protect kidney health and reduce the risk of complications.

  • Take prescribed medicines consistently.
  • Monitor blood pressure and blood glucose when recommended.
  • Follow the recommended eating plan.
  • Avoid smoking.
  • Maintain appropriate physical activity.
  • Avoid unnecessary OTC medicines and herbal products without professional advice.
  • Attend regular kidney function and urine monitoring when recommended.
  • Follow individualized advice about salt, protein, potassium, phosphorus, and fluid intake when dietary modification is required.
Important:

Dietary restrictions in CKD are not identical for everyone. They should be based on kidney function, laboratory results, medications, symptoms, and professional dietary advice.

Kidney Failure & Kidney Replacement Therapy

When kidney function becomes severely reduced, some people may eventually require kidney replacement therapy.

Hemodialysis

Blood is filtered through a dialysis machine to remove waste and excess fluid.

Peritoneal Dialysis

The lining of the abdomen is used as a membrane to help remove waste and excess fluid.

Kidney Transplantation

A functioning donor kidney can replace kidney function in suitable patients.

Some people who cannot or do not want dialysis or transplantation may receive conservative kidney management focused on symptom control and quality of life.

When to Seek Medical Care

Urgent medical assessment may be needed when a person with kidney disease develops serious or rapidly worsening symptoms.

  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Severe or rapidly increasing swelling
  • Very little or no urine
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Severe weakness or confusion
  • Chest pain
  • Seizures or loss of consciousness

Educational Information

This page provides general educational information about chronic kidney disease. Diagnosis and treatment should be individualized by qualified healthcare professionals.

Kidney disease can have different causes and can behave differently from one person to another. Medicine selection, dose adjustment, diet, fluid recommendations, and monitoring should therefore be individualized.

Protect Your Kidneys

Regular monitoring is especially important for people with diabetes, hypertension, or cardiovascular disease.

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Kidney function can influence medicine selection, dosing, monitoring, adverse effects, and treatment safety.

Kidney & Heart Health

Kidney disease and cardiovascular disease are closely connected, making blood pressure and cardiovascular risk management important parts of CKD care.
