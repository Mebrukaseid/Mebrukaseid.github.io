LOWER RESPIRATORY INFECTION

Pneumonia

Understanding pneumonia, its symptoms, causes, diagnosis, treatment, prevention, and medicine safety.

What is pneumonia?

Pneumonia is an acute infection of the lungs. It can affect one or both lungs and may be caused by different microorganisms, including bacteria and viruses.

Pneumonia can range from a mild illness to a serious condition that requires hospital treatment.

Common causes

Pneumonia can have different causes. Common causes include:

  • Bacterial infections
  • Viral infections
  • Respiratory viruses such as influenza and RSV
  • Other microorganisms in particular clinical situations

The cause cannot always be determined from symptoms alone. Healthcare professionals may use clinical assessment and appropriate investigations to help determine the likely cause.

Common symptoms

Pneumonia may cause different symptoms depending on the cause, severity, and characteristics of the individual.

  • Cough
  • Fever or chills
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Chest discomfort or pain
  • Fatigue or weakness
  • Rapid breathing

How is pneumonia diagnosed?

Diagnosis begins with a medical history and physical examination.

Depending on the situation, healthcare professionals may also use measurements such as oxygen saturation and additional investigations to assess the infection and its severity.

Important:

Pneumonia should not be diagnosed only from a list of symptoms. Appropriate clinical assessment is important, especially when symptoms are severe.

Treatment

Treatment of pneumonia depends on the cause, severity, age, underlying health conditions, and clinical assessment.

Bacterial pneumonia

When pneumonia is caused by bacteria, an appropriate antibiotic may be prescribed by a healthcare professional. The choice of antibiotic depends on the patient's condition and the applicable treatment guideline.

Viral pneumonia

Antibiotics do not treat viral infections. Some viral infections may have specific antiviral treatment for selected patients, while supportive care may be appropriate in other cases.

Severe pneumonia

People with severe pneumonia may require treatment in a hospital. This can include oxygen therapy, intravenous medicines, monitoring, and other supportive treatment when clinically indicated.

Important:

Do not start, change, or stop an antibiotic without appropriate medical advice. The wrong antibiotic or unnecessary antibiotic use can cause adverse effects and contribute to antimicrobial resistance.

Home care

Some people with mild pneumonia may be treated at home when a healthcare professional determines that hospital care is not required.

Rest

Get adequate rest and allow the body time to recover. Avoid strenuous physical activity while feeling unwell.

Drink enough fluids

Adequate fluid intake can help prevent dehydration. Water, soups, and other suitable fluids can be used, unless a healthcare professional has advised fluid restriction.

Eat nutritious foods

Try to maintain adequate nutrition during recovery. Small, frequent meals may be easier to tolerate when appetite is reduced.

Follow the treatment plan

If medicines have been prescribed, use them exactly as instructed by the healthcare professional. Do not stop an antibiotic early or change the dose without advice.

Avoid smoke

Avoid cigarette smoke and other sources of indoor air pollution because they can irritate the respiratory system.

Home care does not mean ignoring symptoms.

Pneumonia can become serious. If breathing becomes difficult, symptoms rapidly worsen, or other danger signs develop, seek medical care immediately.

Prevention

Several measures can reduce the risk of respiratory infections and pneumonia.

  • Keep vaccinations up to date when recommended.
  • Wash hands regularly.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are acutely ill when possible.
  • Improve indoor air quality and reduce exposure to harmful smoke.
  • Seek medical care when serious respiratory symptoms develop.

Medicine safety

Pneumonia treatment can involve several medicines depending on the patient's condition. Medication selection should consider allergies, kidney and liver function, age, pregnancy status, comorbidities, and other medicines being taken.

Correct antibiotic selection and use are important because inappropriate antibiotic use can contribute to antimicrobial resistance and adverse effects.

Educational information

This page provides general health education and is not a substitute for professional diagnosis or treatment. Treatment decisions should be made by a qualified healthcare professional.

Seek urgent care

Difficulty breathing, chest pain, bluish lips or face, confusion, severe weakness, or rapidly worsening symptoms require urgent medical assessment.

Remember

Pneumonia can be serious, especially in young children, older adults, and people with certain underlying health conditions.
