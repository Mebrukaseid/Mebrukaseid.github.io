CHRONIC RESPIRATORY DISEASE

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

Understanding COPD, its causes, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, medicines, home care, and ways to protect lung health.

What is COPD?

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a chronic lung condition that causes persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation.

COPD commonly includes conditions such as emphysema and chronic bronchitis. Symptoms can develop gradually and may worsen over time.

Important:

COPD is different from asthma, although some people may have features of both conditions. Proper assessment is important because treatment is not identical.

Causes & Risk Factors

Tobacco smoke is an important risk factor for COPD, but COPD can also occur because of other long-term exposures and individual factors.

  • Cigarette or tobacco smoking
  • Second-hand smoke exposure
  • Household air pollution
  • Occupational exposure to dust, fumes, or chemicals
  • Outdoor air pollution
  • Repeated respiratory infections or other long-term lung problems
  • Genetic conditions such as alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency in selected people

Common Symptoms

COPD symptoms may develop gradually and can become more noticeable as the disease progresses.

  • Shortness of breath
  • Persistent cough
  • Sputum or mucus production
  • Wheezing
  • Chest tightness
  • Reduced exercise tolerance
  • Fatigue
  • Frequent respiratory infections

Diagnosis

COPD should be diagnosed using the person's history, symptoms, physical assessment, and appropriate testing.

Spirometry

Spirometry is an important test used to demonstrate persistent airflow obstruction and support the diagnosis of COPD.

Other assessment

  • Smoking and exposure history
  • Symptom assessment
  • Exacerbation history
  • Oxygen saturation when appropriate
  • Chest imaging when clinically indicated
  • Assessment of other conditions that may contribute to breathing problems

COPD and Its Clinical Features

Chronic Bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis is characterized by chronic productive cough. It may occur as part of the clinical picture of COPD.

Emphysema

Emphysema involves damage to the air sacs of the lungs, reducing the efficiency of gas exchange.

COPD Exacerbation

An exacerbation is a period when respiratory symptoms worsen beyond normal day-to-day variation and may require additional treatment.

COPD Treatment

COPD treatment is individualized according to symptoms, exacerbation history, lung function, smoking status, comorbidities, and treatment response.

Main treatment approaches

  • Smoking cessation
  • Appropriate inhaled medicines
  • Physical activity and pulmonary rehabilitation when appropriate
  • Vaccination and infection prevention
  • Management of exacerbations
  • Management of cardiovascular and other coexisting conditions
  • Oxygen therapy for selected patients with chronic severe hypoxemia

Medicines Used in COPD

Short-Acting Bronchodilators

Short-acting bronchodilators such as salbutamol may provide rapid relief of breathlessness for appropriate patients.

Long-Acting Bronchodilators

Long-acting bronchodilators include long-acting beta-2 agonists (LABAs) and long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMAs).

Examples include formoterol, salmeterol, indacaterol, tiotropium, and umeclidinium.

LABA + LAMA

Combination therapy with a LABA and LAMA may be used when symptoms require more than one long-acting bronchodilator.

Inhaled Corticosteroids

Inhaled corticosteroids may be appropriate for selected patients, particularly when exacerbation risk and other clinical features indicate benefit. They are not automatically required for every person with COPD.

Other medicines

Additional treatments may be considered for selected patients depending on symptoms, exacerbations, mucus production, and other clinical factors.

Inhaler Technique

Correct inhaler technique is essential because incorrect use can significantly reduce the amount of medicine reaching the lungs.

  • Learn the correct technique for the specific inhaler device.
  • Demonstrate inhaler technique to a healthcare professional when possible.
  • Follow the prescribed dosing schedule.
  • Keep inhalers clean and store them correctly.
  • Do not assume different inhalers are used in the same way.
Clinical pharmacy point:

Inhaler technique should be checked regularly, particularly when symptoms remain uncontrolled despite treatment.

COPD Exacerbations

A COPD exacerbation occurs when respiratory symptoms become worse than usual and require additional treatment.

Exacerbations can be triggered by respiratory infections, environmental exposures, and other factors.

Possible symptoms

  • Increased shortness of breath
  • Increased cough
  • Increased sputum production
  • Change in sputum characteristics
  • Reduced ability to perform normal activities

Treatment may include short-acting bronchodilators and, when clinically appropriate, systemic corticosteroids and antibiotics.

Antibiotics are not required for every exacerbation. Clinical assessment is important.

Home Care & Self-Management

Daily self-management can help reduce symptoms, maintain physical activity, and reduce the risk of COPD exacerbations.

  • Stop smoking and avoid second-hand smoke.
  • Use inhalers exactly as prescribed.
  • Check inhaler technique regularly.
  • Avoid known environmental triggers when possible.
  • Maintain appropriate physical activity.
  • Follow pulmonary rehabilitation advice when recommended.
  • Keep vaccinations up to date according to local recommendations.
  • Attend regular follow-up appointments.
  • Learn the person's COPD action plan when one has been provided.
Important:

Increasing breathlessness, worsening cough, increased sputum, or reduced ability to perform usual activities may indicate an exacerbation and should be assessed.

Medicine Safety

  • Use inhalers according to the prescribed plan.
  • Do not use another person's inhaler.
  • Ask before starting new OTC medicines or herbal products.
  • Tell healthcare professionals about all medicines being used.
  • Some medicines can worsen or complicate breathing problems in particular patients.
  • Report troublesome adverse effects.

COPD & Other Health Conditions

COPD commonly occurs together with other chronic conditions.

  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Hypertension
  • Diabetes
  • Anxiety or depression
  • Osteoporosis
  • Lung cancer risk

These conditions should be assessed and managed as part of comprehensive COPD care.

When to Seek Urgent Care

Severe or rapidly worsening breathing problems require urgent medical assessment.

  • Severe difficulty breathing
  • Blue or grey lips or skin
  • New confusion or unusual drowsiness
  • Severe chest pain
  • Inability to speak because of breathlessness
  • Rapidly worsening symptoms despite prescribed treatment
  • Coughing up significant amounts of blood

Educational Information

This page provides general educational information about COPD and does not replace individual medical assessment or treatment.

COPD can vary considerably between individuals. Medicine selection, inhaler choice, treatment intensity, oxygen therapy, and management of exacerbations should be individualized.

Protect Your Lungs

Avoid smoking and harmful inhaled exposures whenever possible and seek assessment for persistent breathing symptoms.

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Inhaler technique, adherence, adverse effects, interactions, and appropriate medicine selection are important parts of COPD care.

Stay Active

Appropriate physical activity and pulmonary rehabilitation can help maintain functional capacity and quality of life.
