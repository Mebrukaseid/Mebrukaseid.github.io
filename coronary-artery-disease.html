CARDIOVASCULAR HEALTH

Coronary Artery Disease

Understanding coronary artery disease, its risk factors, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, medicines, and ways to protect cardiovascular health.

What is Coronary Artery Disease?

Coronary artery disease (CAD), also called ischemic heart disease, occurs when the blood vessels that supply the heart muscle become narrowed or blocked.

The most common cause is atherosclerosis, in which fatty deposits and other substances build up in the walls of the coronary arteries.

Important:

Reduced blood flow to the heart can cause angina and, when a coronary artery becomes suddenly blocked, can result in a heart attack.

Risk Factors

Several factors can increase the risk of coronary artery disease.

  • High blood pressure
  • High LDL cholesterol
  • Diabetes
  • Smoking or tobacco exposure
  • Overweight or obesity
  • Physical inactivity
  • Unhealthy diet
  • Family history of cardiovascular disease
  • Increasing age

Symptoms

Some people with CAD have no symptoms. Others may experience symptoms when the heart needs more oxygen than the narrowed arteries can supply.

  • Chest pressure, tightness, heaviness, or discomfort
  • Shortness of breath
  • Pain or discomfort spreading to the arm, shoulder, back, neck, jaw, or upper abdomen
  • Fatigue
  • Sweating
  • Nausea or dizziness
Remember:

Symptoms can vary between people. Women, older adults, and people with diabetes may sometimes have less typical symptoms.

Angina

Angina is chest discomfort caused by temporary inadequate blood flow and oxygen supply to the heart muscle.

Stable Angina

Symptoms commonly occur with physical activity or emotional stress and improve with rest or prescribed treatment.

Unstable Angina

New, worsening, or unexpected chest discomfort can be a medical emergency because it may be part of acute coronary syndrome.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis depends on symptoms, medical history, physical assessment, cardiovascular risk, and appropriate investigations.

  • Blood pressure measurement
  • Lipid profile
  • Blood glucose or diabetes assessment
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG)
  • Echocardiography when indicated
  • Exercise or other functional testing when appropriate
  • Coronary CT or other imaging in selected patients
  • Coronary angiography when clinically indicated

Treatment & Management

CAD treatment aims to reduce cardiovascular risk, control symptoms, prevent heart attacks, and improve quality of life.

Lifestyle management

  • Stop smoking
  • Maintain appropriate physical activity
  • Follow a heart-healthy eating pattern
  • Maintain a healthy weight when appropriate
  • Manage blood pressure and diabetes
  • Manage cholesterol

Medical treatment

Medicines are selected according to the person's symptoms, cardiovascular risk, previous heart attack, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, kidney function, and other conditions.

Medicines Used in CAD

Antiplatelet Medicines

Aspirin and, in selected situations, other antiplatelet medicines such as clopidogrel may reduce the risk of blood-clot-related cardiovascular events.

Statins

Statins such as atorvastatin and rosuvastatin lower LDL cholesterol and are commonly used for cardiovascular risk reduction.

Beta-Blockers

Medicines such as metoprolol or bisoprolol may be used in selected patients for symptom control or particular cardiovascular indications.

Nitrates

Medicines such as nitroglycerin may be prescribed to relieve or prevent angina in appropriate patients.

ACE Inhibitors and ARBs

These medicines may be particularly useful when CAD occurs together with hypertension, diabetes, heart failure, or other appropriate indications.

Other medicines

Depending on the clinical situation, additional medicines may be used to control heart rate, blood pressure, angina, cholesterol, or other cardiovascular risk factors.

Heart Attack

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle becomes severely reduced or blocked, causing injury to the heart muscle.

A heart attack is a medical emergency and requires immediate medical assessment and treatment.

Do not wait:

New or severe chest pressure, especially when accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, fainting, or pain spreading to the arm, back, neck, or jaw requires emergency medical care.

Procedures & Revascularization

Some people with significant coronary artery disease may require procedures to improve blood flow.

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention

PCI, commonly involving balloon angioplasty and stent placement, can open a narrowed coronary artery in appropriate situations.

Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting

CABG uses blood vessels from other parts of the body to create new pathways around blocked coronary arteries.

The choice between medicines, PCI, CABG, or other approaches depends on the pattern and severity of coronary disease and the individual's clinical situation.

Home Care & Self-Management

Long-term cardiovascular care is important even when symptoms are controlled.

  • Take prescribed cardiovascular medicines consistently.
  • Do not stop antiplatelet medicines or other important medicines without professional advice.
  • Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke.
  • Follow a heart-healthy eating pattern.
  • Maintain appropriate physical activity according to the healthcare team's advice.
  • Monitor blood pressure and blood glucose when recommended.
  • Attend follow-up appointments and laboratory monitoring.
  • Tell healthcare professionals about OTC, herbal, and supplement use.

Medicine Safety

  • Take medicines exactly as prescribed.
  • Ask before using NSAIDs or other OTC pain medicines regularly.
  • Report unusual bleeding when taking antiplatelet medicines.
  • Report unexplained severe muscle pain or weakness while taking a statin.
  • Check for interactions before starting supplements or herbal medicines.
  • Keep an up-to-date list of all medicines.

CAD & Other Conditions

Coronary artery disease commonly occurs together with other chronic conditions.

  • Hypertension
  • Diabetes
  • Chronic kidney disease
  • Heart failure
  • Obesity
  • Dyslipidemia

Managing these conditions together is an important part of reducing cardiovascular risk.

When to Seek Emergency Care

Seek emergency medical care for symptoms that may indicate a heart attack or another serious cardiovascular problem.

  • New or severe chest pressure or pain
  • Chest discomfort lasting several minutes or repeatedly returning
  • Shortness of breath
  • Cold sweating
  • Fainting or severe dizziness
  • Sudden severe weakness
  • Pain spreading to the arm, shoulder, back, neck, or jaw

Educational Information

This page provides general educational information about coronary artery disease and does not replace individual medical assessment or treatment.

Treatment decisions should consider the person's symptoms, cardiovascular risk, previous cardiovascular events, kidney function, bleeding risk, other diseases, and current medicines.

Protect Your Heart

Managing blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, diet, and physical activity can help reduce cardiovascular risk.

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Medication adherence, interactions, adverse effects, cardiovascular risk reduction, and monitoring are important parts of CAD care.

Heart Health

Healthy lifestyle habits and appropriate medical treatment work together to protect cardiovascular health.
