MEDICINE INFORMATION

Aspirin &
Antiplatelet Medicines

Medicines that reduce platelet aggregation and help prevent harmful blood clots in selected cardiovascular and cerebrovascular conditions.

What are antiplatelet medicines?

Antiplatelet medicines reduce the ability of platelets to stick together and form blood clots.

They are commonly used in selected patients with coronary artery disease, acute coronary syndromes, previous myocardial infarction, coronary stents, and some ischemic cerebrovascular conditions.

Important:

Antiplatelet medicines are not appropriate for everyone. The reason for treatment and the risk of bleeding must be considered before therapy is started or changed.

1. Aspirin

Aspirin inhibits platelet aggregation by irreversibly inhibiting platelet cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1). This reduces thromboxane A2 production and decreases platelet activation.

Common uses

  • Secondary prevention after myocardial infarction
  • Selected patients with established coronary artery disease
  • Acute coronary syndromes
  • Patients undergoing selected coronary interventions

In chronic coronary syndromes, aspirin or clopidogrel may be used as long-term single antiplatelet therapy depending on the patient's clinical situation.

Do not self-start daily aspirin:

Daily aspirin should not be started simply because a person wants to prevent a heart attack. The potential cardiovascular benefit must be balanced against bleeding risk.

2. Clopidogrel

Clopidogrel is a P2Y12 receptor inhibitor. It reduces ADP-mediated platelet activation and aggregation.

Common uses

  • Acute coronary syndromes
  • After selected coronary stent procedures
  • Secondary prevention in selected patients with ischemic stroke or TIA
  • Alternative antiplatelet therapy when aspirin is not suitable in selected patients

Clopidogrel is frequently used together with aspirin for a period of time in appropriate patients. This is called dual antiplatelet therapy.

3. Ticagrelor

Ticagrelor is a reversible P2Y12 platelet inhibitor that produces more rapid platelet inhibition than clopidogrel.

It is commonly considered in patients with acute coronary syndromes, particularly when dual antiplatelet therapy is indicated.

Important adverse effects

  • Bleeding
  • Shortness of breath (dyspnea)
  • Occasional bradyarrhythmia-related effects

4. Prasugrel

Prasugrel is another P2Y12 inhibitor used in selected patients with acute coronary syndrome undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).

Important safety point:

Prasugrel is generally avoided in patients with a previous stroke or transient ischemic attack because of increased bleeding risk. Patient-specific factors must be considered before use.

Dual Antiplatelet Therapy

Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) means using aspirin together with a P2Y12 inhibitor.

Common combinations

  • Aspirin + clopidogrel
  • Aspirin + ticagrelor
  • Aspirin + prasugrel

DAPT is commonly used after acute coronary syndromes and in selected patients after coronary stent implantation.

The duration depends on the clinical indication, ischemic risk, bleeding risk, procedures performed, and other patient factors.

Never stop DAPT suddenly without medical advice.

In patients with coronary stents, stopping antiplatelet therapy without appropriate medical guidance can increase the risk of serious cardiovascular events.

Common Adverse Effects

The major safety concern with antiplatelet medicines is bleeding.

  • Easy bruising
  • Nosebleeds
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Prolonged bleeding after injury
  • Rarely, serious internal or intracranial bleeding

Aspirin-specific concerns

  • Gastric irritation
  • Peptic ulcer complications
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Bronchospasm in susceptible individuals

Important Drug Interactions

Antiplatelet medicines can interact with other medicines and increase bleeding risk.

  • Anticoagulants such as warfarin and heparins
  • Other antiplatelet medicines
  • NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen
  • Other medicines that increase bleeding risk

Clopidogrel and proton-pump inhibitors

Clopidogrel requires activation through CYP2C19. Medicines that substantially inhibit this pathway can reduce clopidogrel activation. The choice of acid suppression should therefore be considered carefully when clinically needed.

When to Seek Urgent Help

A person taking an antiplatelet medicine should seek urgent medical attention for signs of significant bleeding.

  • Vomiting blood
  • Black or bloody stools
  • Heavy bleeding that does not stop
  • Sudden severe headache
  • Sudden weakness, confusion, or difficulty speaking
  • Fainting or severe dizziness associated with bleeding

Special Considerations

Older adults

Bleeding risk may increase with age, especially when several antithrombotic medicines are used together.

History of gastrointestinal bleeding

Patients with previous gastrointestinal bleeding may require additional assessment and, in appropriate situations, gastroprotective treatment.

Asthma

Some people with asthma, particularly those with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease, can develop respiratory reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs.

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Clinical pharmacy care includes checking the indication for antiplatelet therapy, assessing bleeding risk, identifying drug interactions, monitoring adherence, and educating patients about warning signs.

When aspirin is combined with a P2Y12 inhibitor, the indication and intended duration of dual antiplatelet therapy should be clearly documented.

Patient Education

  • Take the medicine exactly as prescribed.
  • Do not stop antiplatelet therapy without medical advice.
  • Tell healthcare professionals that an antiplatelet medicine is being taken.
  • Ask before using NSAIDs or other medicines that may increase bleeding.
  • Report unusual or significant bleeding promptly.
Remember:

Antiplatelet medicines prevent harmful platelet clotting, but they also increase bleeding risk. The benefit and risk must always be considered together.

Bleeding Risk

Unusual or significant bleeding while taking an antiplatelet medicine requires prompt medical assessment.

Heart Protection

Antiplatelet therapy can help prevent harmful blood clots in selected cardiovascular conditions.
