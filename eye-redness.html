COMMON CONDITION

Understanding Eye Redness

Eye redness can have many causes, from simple irritation and dry eyes to infections and other conditions that need medical assessment.

THE BASICS

What is eye redness?

Eye redness happens when the small blood vessels on the surface of the eye become more visible or enlarged. It can affect one or both eyes.

Redness itself is a symptom rather than a diagnosis. The cause may be minor irritation, dryness, allergy, infection, an eyelid problem, an injury, or another eye condition.

Because different eye conditions can look similar, persistent or concerning symptoms should be assessed by an appropriate healthcare professional.

COMMON CAUSES

Why can the eye become red?

Several different conditions can cause redness. The accompanying symptoms can provide useful clues, but self-diagnosis should be avoided.

Dry Eyes

Dryness can cause burning, irritation, watering, a gritty feeling, blurred vision, and redness.

Conjunctivitis

Conjunctivitis may cause redness, irritation, watering, and sometimes sticky discharge.

Allergies

Allergic irritation can cause red, itchy, watery eyes, particularly after exposure to a trigger.

Blepharitis

Inflammation around the eyelids can cause redness, irritation, crusting, or sore eyelids.

Burst Blood Vessel

A small blood vessel can sometimes break and produce a bright red patch on the white part of the eye.

Irritation or Injury

Dust, chemicals, foreign objects, rubbing, or other injuries can irritate or damage the eye.

WHAT TO NOTICE

Redness may occur with other symptoms.

Watery Eyes

Excessive watering may occur with irritation, dryness, allergies, or infection.

Itching or Irritation

Itching and irritation are common with allergies and some forms of conjunctivitis.

Blurred Vision

Changes in vision are more concerning and should not simply be assumed to be caused by minor irritation.

Light Sensitivity

Pain or significant sensitivity to light can be a warning sign of a more serious eye problem.

SAFE SELF-CARE

What can you do for a mild red eye?

01

Do Not Rub the Eye

Avoid rubbing or pressing the eye because this can increase irritation or potentially cause further injury.

02

Keep Hands Clean

Wash hands regularly, especially before touching the face or using any eye product.

03

Avoid Contact Lenses

If the eye is red, avoid wearing contact lenses until the problem has improved and it is safe to resume use.

04

Reduce Irritation

Avoid smoke, dust, and other known irritants when possible and give irritated eyes a chance to recover.

MEDICATION SAFETY

Be careful with eye drops.

Not every red eye needs the same medicine.

Eye drops are chosen according to the underlying cause. Lubricating drops may be useful for some dry-eye symptoms, while infections, allergies, inflammation, or injuries may require different approaches.

Antibiotic or steroid eye drops should not be used simply because an eye is red. The correct treatment depends on the cause and should be determined by an appropriate healthcare professional.

Avoid using leftover prescription eye drops or someone else's eye medicine.

CONTACT LENS USERS

A red eye with contact lenses needs attention.

Do not ignore a painful red eye.

Contact lens wear can be associated with serious eye infections. If a contact lens wearer develops a red or painful eye, especially with reduced or blurred vision, prompt professional assessment is important.

Remove contact lenses and do not continue wearing them until a healthcare professional advises that it is safe.

IMPORTANT

When should eye redness be assessed urgently?

Some symptoms can indicate a more serious eye problem.

Vision Changes

Blurred vision, loss of vision, flashing lights, or other sudden changes in sight.

Severe Eye Pain

Significant or severe pain together with eye redness requires prompt assessment.

Light Sensitivity

Pain when looking at light or marked sensitivity to light can be a warning sign.

Severe Headache or Nausea

A red eye together with severe headache and feeling sick needs urgent medical attention.

Eye Injury

Injury, a penetrating object, or something stuck in the eye requires appropriate assessment.

Chemical Exposure

Chemical exposure to the eye is an emergency and requires immediate first-aid and medical attention.

HEALTH AWARENESS

Red eyes are common, but warning signs matter.

Many cases of eye redness are mild and improve, but pain, vision changes, light sensitivity, injury, chemical exposure, or a painful red eye in a contact lens wearer should not be ignored.

Health information disclaimer

This page provides general health-awareness information for educational purposes. It does not diagnose or treat an individual condition and should not replace professional medical or eye-care advice.

If symptoms are severe, persistent, worsening, or associated with vision changes, seek appropriate medical attention.
