MEDICATION SAFETY

Understanding Drug Interactions.

Medicines do not always act independently. Their effects can be influenced by other medicines, foods, herbal products, alcohol, diseases, and individual patient factors.

Explore Interactions Back to Home

WHY IT MATTERS

Medicines can interact in different ways.

A drug interaction happens when one medicine, food, supplement, herbal product, disease, or other factor changes the effect or handling of another medicine.

Some interactions may reduce treatment effectiveness, while others can increase adverse effects or toxicity.

01 • DRUG INTERACTION

Drug–Drug Interactions

Drug–drug interactions occur when one medicine changes the effect, concentration, absorption, metabolism, or elimination of another medicine.

EXAMPLE

Warfarin + NSAIDs

Combining anticoagulants such as warfarin with NSAIDs may increase bleeding risk.

Check for bleeding risk before combining medicines that affect hemostasis.
EXAMPLE

ACE inhibitor + Diuretic + NSAID

This combination can increase the risk of kidney injury in susceptible patients.

Kidney function and other risk factors may need monitoring.
EXAMPLE

Medicines that prolong QT

Combining several medicines that prolong the QT interval may increase the risk of serious cardiac arrhythmias.

Medication lists should be reviewed for additive cardiac effects.
EXAMPLE

Multiple Sedating Medicines

Combining medicines that cause sedation can increase drowsiness and impair alertness.

Extra caution may be needed with activities requiring alertness.

Enzyme interactions

Some medicines affect drug-metabolizing enzymes such as CYP enzymes. Enzyme induction can increase metabolism of some medicines, while enzyme inhibition can reduce metabolism and increase drug exposure.

Common Enzyme Inducers

  • Rifampicin
  • Carbamazepine
  • Phenytoin
  • Phenobarbital
  • St John's wort

Common Enzyme Inhibitors

  • Clarithromycin
  • Erythromycin
  • Azole antifungals
  • Ritonavir
  • Grapefruit products

02 • DRUG INTERACTION

Drug–Food Interactions

Food and beverages can affect medicine absorption, metabolism, effectiveness, or adverse effects. The effect depends on the particular medicine.

EXAMPLE

Grapefruit

Grapefruit and grapefruit juice can affect the metabolism of certain medicines and may increase their exposure.

Check the medicine-specific information before advising grapefruit avoidance.
EXAMPLE

Vitamin K + Warfarin

Changes in vitamin K intake can affect anticoagulation with warfarin.

Consistency of dietary vitamin K intake is important for patients taking warfarin.
EXAMPLE

Minerals + Medicines

Calcium, iron, magnesium, and other minerals can reduce absorption of certain medicines.

Some medicines need to be separated from mineral supplements.

03 • PATIENT FACTORS

Drug–Disease Interactions

A medicine may worsen an existing disease or may require additional monitoring because of a patient's underlying condition.

EXAMPLE

NSAIDs + Kidney Disease

NSAIDs can affect kidney function and may require particular caution in patients with kidney disease.

EXAMPLE

NSAIDs + Heart Failure

NSAIDs may promote fluid retention and can complicate management in some patients with heart failure.

EXAMPLE

Beta Blockers + Asthma

Some beta blockers can affect bronchial function, so the choice of beta blocker matters in patients with asthma.

EXAMPLE

Potassium-Raising Medicines

Several medicines can increase potassium. Combining them may increase the risk of hyperkalemia in susceptible patients.

EXAMPLE

Decongestants + Hypertension

Some systemic decongestants can increase blood pressure and may require caution in people with hypertension.

04 • HERBAL MEDICINES

Herbal Products + Medicines

“Natural” does not automatically mean “interaction-free.” Herbal products can contain active substances that affect drug metabolism, absorption, or pharmacologic effects.

IMPORTANT EXAMPLE

St John's Wort

St John's wort can affect drug-metabolizing enzymes and transporters and may reduce the effectiveness of some medicines.

PATIENT SAFETY

Ask About Herbal Products

Patients may not always consider herbal products to be medicines, so they may not mention them unless specifically asked.

IMPORTANT

Traditional Medicines

Traditional and herbal products should be considered when reviewing a patient's complete medication history.

Complete medication history

Tell the healthcare professional about prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, supplements, herbal products, and traditional medicines.

05 • ALCOHOL

Medicines + Alcohol

Alcohol can interact with medicines in several ways. It may increase sedation, worsen certain adverse effects, or affect the way some medicines are handled by the body.

Sedating Medicines

Alcohol can add to the sedating effects of medicines that cause drowsiness.

NSAIDs + Alcohol

Alcohol may increase gastrointestinal irritation and other adverse effects associated with NSAIDs.

Aspirin + Alcohol

Alcohol can increase some of the risks associated with aspirin, including gastrointestinal adverse effects and bleeding.

Check before combining

If a medicine has a warning about alcohol, follow the medicine-specific advice and ask a pharmacist or healthcare professional when uncertain.

PRACTICAL MEDICATION SAFETY

Small details can make a difference.

01

Follow the prescribed dose

Do not increase, decrease, or stop a medicine without appropriate medical advice.

02

Keep a medication list

Keep track of prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, supplements, and herbal products.

03

Ask before adding a medicine

Check with a pharmacist or healthcare professional before adding a new medicine or supplement.

04

Do not double missed doses

The correct response to a missed dose depends on the medicine. Check the medicine information or ask a healthcare professional.

05

Read medicine instructions

Pay attention to instructions about food, timing, storage, crushing tablets, and other administration requirements.

06

Report unusual reactions

Seek appropriate medical advice when a new, severe, or unexpected reaction occurs after starting a medicine.

EXTRA CAUTION

Some situations require closer medication review.

Kidney disease

Some medicines require dose adjustment or additional monitoring when kidney function is reduced.

Liver disease

Liver dysfunction can affect the metabolism of certain medicines.

Pregnancy

Medication decisions during pregnancy should consider both maternal and fetal safety.

Older adults

Multiple medicines, altered drug handling, and increased sensitivity can increase medication-related risks.

Polypharmacy

Taking multiple medicines increases the opportunity for drug interactions and medication-related problems.

CLINICAL PHARMACY APPROACH

Think beyond the medicine name.

Reviewing medication safety involves looking at the complete patient and medication profile, not only one medicine at a time.

Medication List

Review all prescription, OTC, herbal, and supplement products.

Patient Factors

Consider age, kidney function, liver function, pregnancy, allergies, and comorbidities.

Timing

Consider dose intervals, meal timing, administration instructions, and adherence.

Monitoring

Identify clinical and laboratory parameters that may need monitoring.

HEALTH AWARENESS

Never hesitate to ask about your medicines.

If you are unsure about when to take a medicine, whether it can be taken with food, or whether another medicine or supplement could interact with it, ask a pharmacist or other qualified healthcare professional.
