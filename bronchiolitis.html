PEDIATRIC RESPIRATORY HEALTH

Bronchiolitis

Understanding bronchiolitis in babies and young children, including causes, symptoms, supportive care, medicine safety, and warning signs.

What is bronchiolitis?

Bronchiolitis is a common infection that causes inflammation and swelling of the small airways in the lungs.

It mainly affects babies and young children, especially those younger than two years of age.

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a common cause, although other respiratory viruses can also cause bronchiolitis.

Common symptoms

Bronchiolitis often begins like an ordinary cold and may then affect breathing.

  • Runny or blocked nose
  • Sneezing
  • Cough
  • Faster breathing
  • Wheezing or noisy breathing
  • Difficulty feeding
  • Reduced appetite
  • Irritability
  • Tiredness
  • Fever may occur

Causes

Respiratory viruses

Bronchiolitis is usually caused by a viral respiratory infection.

Respiratory syncytial virus

RSV is one of the most common causes of bronchiolitis in young children.

Other respiratory viruses

Other viruses can also cause bronchiolitis, including viruses responsible for common respiratory infections.

Important:

Because bronchiolitis is usually viral, antibiotics do not routinely treat the underlying infection.

Who may be at higher risk?

Bronchiolitis can affect any young child, but some children have a greater risk of developing severe illness.

  • Very young infants
  • Babies born prematurely
  • Children with chronic lung disease
  • Children with certain heart conditions
  • Children with weakened immune systems
  • Children with certain neuromuscular conditions

The younger the child, the greater the concern for severe respiratory illness in general.

Diagnosis

Bronchiolitis is usually diagnosed from the child's symptoms and clinical examination.

A healthcare professional may assess breathing rate, breathing effort, oxygenation, hydration, and the child's ability to feed.

Routine laboratory tests and chest X-rays are not usually necessary in typical bronchiolitis, although additional testing may be considered when the illness is atypical or another diagnosis is suspected.

Treatment

There is usually no specific medicine that cures uncomplicated bronchiolitis. Treatment mainly consists of supportive care.

Fluids and feeding

Maintaining hydration is important. Babies who have difficulty feeding may need smaller amounts more frequently.

Oxygen

Children with significant breathing difficulty or low oxygen levels may require oxygen therapy in a healthcare facility.

Hospital treatment

Severe bronchiolitis may require hospital care. Treatment can include oxygen, fluid support, and close monitoring of breathing and hydration.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics do not treat the viruses that usually cause bronchiolitis and should not be used routinely.

Other medicines

Medicines such as bronchodilators and corticosteroids are not routinely recommended for uncomplicated bronchiolitis unless another clinical condition provides a specific indication.

Important:

Treatment decisions for infants and young children should be made by an appropriate healthcare professional. Do not give a child medicines without checking that they are appropriate for the child's age and condition.

Home care

Many children with mild bronchiolitis can be cared for at home with close observation.

Give small, frequent feeds

A baby may become tired while feeding because of nasal congestion or faster breathing. Smaller, more frequent feeds can help maintain fluid intake.

Keep the nose clear

Saline nasal drops may help loosen nasal secretions and make breathing and feeding more comfortable.

Keep the child comfortable

Allow the child to rest and monitor the child regularly, including during the night.

Avoid smoke

Do not smoke around a child with bronchiolitis. Tobacco smoke can worsen respiratory symptoms and increase the risk of serious respiratory illness.

Monitor feeding and wet nappies

Pay attention to how much the child drinks and how often the child urinates. Reduced urine can be a sign of dehydration.

Remember:

A child who is drinking much less than usual, has significantly fewer wet nappies, or is becoming increasingly tired needs medical assessment.

Medicine safety

  • Do not give antibiotics unless prescribed or specifically recommended by a healthcare professional.
  • Do not give aspirin to children.
  • Check the age restrictions before giving any over-the-counter medicine to a child.
  • Do not use adult medicines for children unless specifically instructed by a healthcare professional.
  • Avoid combining medicines that contain the same active ingredient.
  • Ask a pharmacist or healthcare professional before giving cough and cold medicines to young children.
Special caution:

Young children are more vulnerable to medicine errors and adverse effects. Always check the medicine, strength, age suitability, and dose instructions carefully.

Prevention

Respiratory viruses can spread easily between people. Several measures can reduce transmission.

  • Wash hands regularly with soap and water.
  • Avoid close contact between babies and people who are sick when possible.
  • Clean frequently touched surfaces.
  • Avoid exposing babies and young children to cigarette smoke.
  • Follow locally recommended childhood immunization guidance.

When to seek medical care

Bronchiolitis can become serious, especially in very young infants. Seek medical advice when a child's symptoms are worsening or feeding becomes difficult.

  • Breathing faster than usual
  • Increasing difficulty breathing
  • Ribs or stomach pulling in during breathing
  • Grunting while breathing
  • Feeding much less than normal
  • Significantly fewer wet nappies
  • Increasing tiredness or unusual sleepiness
  • Symptoms getting worse
Emergency:

Pauses in breathing, severe difficulty breathing, blue or grey lips or skin, or a child who is difficult to wake requires emergency medical attention.

Educational information

This page provides general health education about bronchiolitis. It does not replace professional examination, diagnosis, or individualized treatment.

Because bronchiolitis mainly affects infants and young children, caregivers should seek professional medical advice whenever there is concern about breathing, feeding, hydration, or worsening symptoms.

Watch breathing

Increasing breathing difficulty, pauses in breathing, blue or grey lips, or severe tiredness requires urgent medical attention.

Important for babies

Bronchiolitis mainly affects babies and young children. Feeding and hydration should be monitored carefully during illness.
