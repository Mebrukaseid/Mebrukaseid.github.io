UPPER RESPIRATORY INFECTION

Common Cold

Understanding common cold symptoms, causes, treatment, home care, prevention, and safe medicine use.

What is the common cold?

The common cold is a common infection affecting the upper respiratory tract. It is usually caused by viruses.

Most colds are mild and improve with time. Different viruses can cause cold-like symptoms, so having a cold does not necessarily mean infection with one specific virus.

Common symptoms

Common cold symptoms may include:

  • Runny or blocked nose
  • Sneezing
  • Sore throat
  • Cough
  • Mild headache
  • Mild body aches
  • Feeling tired or unwell

Symptoms can vary from person to person and may overlap with other respiratory infections.

What causes a common cold?

The common cold can be caused by many different respiratory viruses.

These viruses can spread through respiratory droplets and contaminated hands or surfaces.

Treatment

There is usually no specific medicine that cures an ordinary common cold. Treatment mainly focuses on relieving symptoms while the body recovers.

Symptom relief

Depending on the symptoms, supportive measures and appropriate over-the-counter medicines may help provide relief.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics do not treat the viruses that cause the common cold. They should not be used simply because someone has a cold.

Important:

If symptoms are severe, unusual, persistent, or getting worse, medical assessment may be needed to determine whether another condition is present.

Home care

Most people with a mild common cold can recover with supportive care.

Rest

Get enough rest and avoid excessive physical activity while feeling unwell.

Fluids

Drink adequate fluids to help maintain hydration.

Warm fluids

Warm drinks such as water, tea, or soup may help soothe a sore throat and provide comfort.

Healthy food

Continue eating nutritious foods as tolerated during recovery.

Avoid smoke

Avoid cigarette smoke and other respiratory irritants while recovering.

Remember:

Home remedies may provide comfort, but they do not replace medical treatment when a serious respiratory condition is present.

Medicine safety

Many cold products contain more than one active ingredient. Taking several products at the same time can accidentally result in taking the same medicine more than once.

Always check the active ingredients and follow the instructions on the product label or advice from a healthcare professional.

Children

Medicine selection and dosing for children require particular care. Medicines should not be given to children simply because they are used by adults.

Prevention

  • Wash hands regularly with soap and water.
  • Avoid touching the eyes, nose, and mouth with unwashed hands.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are sick when possible.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes appropriately.
  • Keep frequently touched surfaces clean.

When to seek medical care

A cold is usually mild, but medical assessment may be appropriate when symptoms are severe or concerning.

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Chest pain
  • Severe or worsening symptoms
  • Significant dehydration
  • Symptoms that do not improve as expected

Educational information

This page provides general health education and does not replace diagnosis, prescribing, or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional.

Important

A common cold is usually mild, but breathing difficulty, chest pain, severe weakness, or rapidly worsening symptoms require medical assessment.

Home care

Rest, adequate fluids, nutritious food, and symptom relief can help support recovery from a mild cold.
