RESPIRATORY HEALTH

Laryngitis

Understanding laryngitis, its causes, symptoms, treatment approaches, home care, and medicine safety.

What is laryngitis?

Laryngitis is inflammation or irritation of the larynx, also called the voice box. The vocal cords are located inside the larynx and normally vibrate to produce the voice.

When the vocal cords become swollen or irritated, the voice may become hoarse, weak, or temporarily disappear.

Acute laryngitis commonly improves on its own within about one to two weeks.

Common symptoms

Common symptoms may include:

  • Hoarse or raspy voice
  • Weak voice
  • Temporary loss of voice
  • Sore or irritated throat
  • Dry or irritating cough
  • Frequent need to clear the throat
  • Difficulty speaking normally

Common causes

Viral infections

Viral respiratory infections, including common colds and influenza, are common causes of acute laryngitis.

Voice strain

Excessive speaking, shouting, singing, or other activities that place stress on the voice can irritate the vocal cords.

Smoke and irritants

Cigarette smoke, secondhand smoke, dust, fumes, and other irritants can contribute to irritation of the larynx.

Acid reflux

Stomach acid that repeatedly reaches the throat can irritate the larynx and contribute to voice problems.

Allergies

Allergies and exposure to certain environmental irritants may also contribute to throat and voice irritation.

Diagnosis

Laryngitis is often assessed from the person's symptoms and a physical examination.

A healthcare professional may examine the throat and determine whether another condition could be causing the symptoms.

If voice problems keep returning or do not improve, further assessment may be required. In some cases, referral to an ear, nose and throat specialist may be appropriate.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause of laryngitis. Most uncomplicated acute cases improve with supportive care.

Rest the voice

Speaking less can reduce irritation and give the vocal cords time to recover.

Maintain hydration

Drinking adequate fluids can help maintain hydration while the throat and voice recover.

Treat the underlying cause

When laryngitis is related to an underlying condition such as reflux, allergies, or another respiratory infection, management may focus on the underlying cause.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics do not treat viral infections. Therefore, antibiotics are not routinely useful for uncomplicated viral laryngitis.

If a healthcare professional identifies a bacterial infection or another condition requiring specific treatment, appropriate treatment can be selected based on the clinical assessment.

Important:

Do not start antibiotics simply because the voice is hoarse or has been lost. Most acute laryngitis is associated with causes that do not require antibiotics.

Home care

Several simple measures can help reduce discomfort while the voice recovers.

Rest the voice

Speak as little as reasonably possible while symptoms are present. Avoid shouting and prolonged speaking.

Avoid whispering

Whispering can also place strain on the voice. Use a gentle normal voice when speaking is necessary.

Drink enough fluids

Drink adequate water and other suitable fluids throughout the day.

Keep the air comfortable

Dry air may irritate the throat. Maintaining comfortable humidity can help reduce irritation.

Warm drinks

Warm drinks may provide soothing relief for an irritated throat.

Avoid smoke and dust

Avoid cigarette smoke, secondhand smoke, dusty environments, and other respiratory irritants.

Avoid excessive alcohol and caffeine

Excessive alcohol and caffeine intake may contribute to dehydration. Adequate fluid intake is important during recovery.

Remember:

Home care is intended to support recovery and relieve symptoms. It does not replace medical assessment when symptoms are severe or persistent.

Medicine safety

  • Do not use antibiotics without appropriate medical advice.
  • Check the active ingredients in combination cold and flu products.
  • Avoid taking multiple products containing the same pain-relieving ingredient.
  • Children should receive age-appropriate medicines according to professional advice and product instructions.
  • People who are pregnant, elderly, or living with chronic diseases should ask a healthcare professional before using medicines.
  • Tell a healthcare professional about other medicines being taken.

Prevention

Prevention depends partly on the cause of the laryngitis.

  • Wash hands regularly.
  • Avoid close contact with people who have contagious respiratory infections when possible.
  • Cover the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing.
  • Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke.
  • Avoid excessive voice strain.
  • Reduce exposure to dust, fumes, and other throat irritants.

When to seek medical care

Medical assessment is recommended when symptoms are severe, persistent, recurrent, or getting worse.

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms
  • Inability to swallow fluids
  • Significant dehydration
  • Voice problems that do not improve after about two weeks
  • Repeated episodes of laryngitis or persistent voice problems
Emergency:

Difficulty breathing or severe difficulty swallowing requires urgent medical attention.

Educational information

This page provides general health education about laryngitis. It does not replace professional examination, diagnosis, or individualized treatment.

If symptoms are severe, persistent, unusual, or worsening, seek assessment from a qualified healthcare professional.

Seek urgent care

Difficulty breathing or severe difficulty swallowing requires urgent medical attention.
