UPPER RESPIRATORY INFECTION

Pharyngitis

Understanding sore throat, its causes, symptoms, treatment, home care, prevention, and safe medicine use.

What is pharyngitis?

Pharyngitis is inflammation of the pharynx, the area of the throat behind the mouth and nasal cavity.

It commonly presents as a sore throat. Pharyngitis can be caused by viruses or, in some cases, bacteria.

Common causes

Pharyngitis can have several causes. Common causes include:

  • Viral respiratory infections
  • Streptococcal bacterial infection in some patients
  • Other infections
  • Irritation from smoke or other environmental factors
  • Allergic or inflammatory conditions

The cause is important because treatment differs between viral and bacterial infections.

Common symptoms

Pharyngitis may cause:

  • Sore throat
  • Pain when swallowing
  • Red or irritated throat
  • Fever
  • Swollen lymph nodes in the neck
  • Headache
  • General tiredness

Cough and runny nose may occur when pharyngitis is associated with a viral respiratory infection.

Viral and bacterial pharyngitis

Viral pharyngitis

Viral infections are a common cause of sore throat. They often occur together with symptoms such as cough, runny nose, or other cold-like symptoms.

Bacterial pharyngitis

Some cases are caused by bacteria such as group A streptococcus. Appropriate clinical assessment and, when indicated, testing can help determine whether bacterial infection is likely.

Important:

A sore throat alone does not mean that an antibiotic is needed.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis may involve a medical history and examination of the throat and neck.

When bacterial infection is suspected, healthcare professionals may use appropriate tests to help guide treatment.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and severity of the condition.

Viral pharyngitis

Viral pharyngitis is generally managed with supportive care and treatment aimed at relieving symptoms.

Bacterial pharyngitis

When a bacterial infection such as streptococcal pharyngitis is confirmed or considered sufficiently likely, appropriate antibiotic treatment may be prescribed by a healthcare professional.

Symptom relief

Depending on the individual and symptoms, appropriate medicines or other supportive measures may help reduce pain or fever.

Medicine safety:

Do not use leftover antibiotics or antibiotics prescribed for another person. Unnecessary antibiotic use can cause adverse effects and contribute to antimicrobial resistance.

Home care

Mild pharyngitis can often be supported with simple measures while the underlying infection improves.

Drink fluids

Drink adequate fluids to maintain hydration and help soothe throat discomfort.

Warm drinks

Warm water, tea, or soup may provide temporary relief from throat discomfort.

Soft foods

Soft foods may be easier to eat when swallowing is painful.

Rest

Adequate rest can support recovery from respiratory infections.

Avoid smoke

Avoid cigarette smoke and other irritants that may worsen throat irritation.

Prevention

  • Wash hands regularly.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes.
  • Avoid sharing drinking glasses and eating utensils with someone who is sick.
  • Avoid close contact with people who have an active respiratory infection.
  • Avoid smoking and exposure to secondhand smoke.

When to seek medical care

Medical assessment is important when throat symptoms are severe or associated with concerning signs.

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Difficulty swallowing liquids
  • Drooling or inability to swallow
  • Severe swelling of the throat or neck
  • Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms
  • Persistent high fever

Educational information

This page provides general health education and does not replace diagnosis, prescribing, or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional.

Important

Severe difficulty breathing or swallowing, drooling, or rapidly worsening throat swelling requires urgent medical assessment.

Home care

Fluids, warm drinks, rest, soft foods, and avoiding smoke may help provide comfort during recovery.
