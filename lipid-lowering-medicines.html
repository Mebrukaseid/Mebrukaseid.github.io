MEDICINE INFORMATION

Lipid-Lowering
Medicines

Medicines used to reduce cholesterol and triglycerides and help lower the risk of cardiovascular disease. This page focuses on commonly used lipid-lowering medicines and important safety points.

What are lipid-lowering medicines?

Lipid-lowering medicines are drugs used to reduce unhealthy levels of cholesterol or triglycerides in the blood.

They are commonly used together with healthy eating, physical activity, weight management when appropriate, and other measures that reduce cardiovascular risk.

Important:

The choice of medicine depends on the patient's lipid levels, cardiovascular risk, medical conditions, other medicines, and treatment goals.

1. Statins

Statins are the main group of medicines used to lower LDL cholesterol. They reduce cholesterol production in the liver and help reduce the risk of cardiovascular events.

Common examples

  • Atorvastatin
  • Rosuvastatin
  • Simvastatin
  • Pravastatin

Atorvastatin

Atorvastatin is a commonly used statin for lowering LDL cholesterol. It is available in several strengths and may be prescribed for people with elevated cholesterol or increased cardiovascular risk.

Rosuvastatin

Rosuvastatin is another commonly used statin with strong LDL-lowering activity. The appropriate dose depends on the patient's treatment goal and clinical situation.

Common adverse effects:

Muscle aches can occur. Rarely, serious muscle injury can occur. Liver enzyme abnormalities may also occur.

2. Ezetimibe

Ezetimibe reduces the absorption of cholesterol from the intestine and lowers LDL cholesterol.

It may be used alone in selected patients or added to a statin when additional LDL reduction is needed or when a patient cannot tolerate an adequate statin regimen.

Important points

  • Primarily lowers LDL cholesterol.
  • Can be combined with a statin.
  • Usually has relatively few adverse effects.
  • Liver-related monitoring may be relevant when used with a statin.

3. Fibrates

Fibrates are mainly used to lower triglyceride levels. They are particularly relevant when triglycerides are markedly elevated.

Common examples

  • Fenofibrate
  • Gemfibrozil

Important points

Fibrates are not usually the main medicines for elevated LDL cholesterol. Their major role is triglyceride lowering.

Drug interaction:

Gemfibrozil can significantly increase the risk of statin-associated muscle toxicity when combined with certain statins. This combination requires particular caution.

4. Prescription Omega-3 Medicines

Prescription omega-3 medicines can be used to lower elevated triglycerides in selected patients.

Common examples

  • Icosapent ethyl
  • Prescription omega-3 acid ethyl esters

Prescription omega-3 medicines should not be confused with ordinary over-the-counter fish-oil supplements. Their indications, doses, and evidence may differ.

Monitoring and Safety

Lipid-lowering therapy should be monitored according to the medicine used and the patient's clinical situation.

Important monitoring points

  • Lipid profile and response to treatment
  • Adherence to treatment
  • Muscle symptoms with statins
  • Liver-related concerns when clinically indicated
  • Kidney function for selected medicines such as fibrates
  • Potential drug-drug interactions

Lifestyle and Cholesterol

Medicines are only one part of cardiovascular risk reduction. Healthy lifestyle measures remain important even when lipid-lowering medicines are prescribed.

  • Choose a heart-healthy eating pattern.
  • Limit saturated and trans fats.
  • Maintain regular physical activity.
  • Avoid tobacco exposure.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight when appropriate.
  • Take prescribed medicines consistently.

When to Seek Medical Advice

A person taking a lipid-lowering medicine should seek medical advice if new or concerning symptoms develop.

  • Unexplained severe muscle pain or weakness
  • Dark-colored urine with significant muscle symptoms
  • Yellowing of the skin or eyes
  • Severe or persistent abdominal symptoms
  • Possible allergic reaction

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Clinical pharmacy care can help identify the appropriate lipid-lowering medicine, assess adherence, monitor treatment response, identify drug interactions, and recognize adverse drug reactions.

Special attention may be needed when lipid-lowering medicines are used in patients with diabetes, chronic kidney disease, liver disease, cardiovascular disease, or multiple medications.

Patient Education

Lipid-lowering medicines are often used for long-term cardiovascular risk reduction. Patients should take medicines as prescribed and should not stop treatment without discussing it with a healthcare professional.

Remember:

A cholesterol medicine should be selected according to the individual's clinical situation rather than simply choosing the medicine with the strongest effect.

Medicine Safety

Do not start, stop, or change a lipid-lowering medicine without appropriate medical advice.

Heart Health

Managing cholesterol is one part of reducing cardiovascular risk. Lifestyle and management of other risk factors are also important.
