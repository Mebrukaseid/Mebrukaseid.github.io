MEDICINE INFORMATION

Heart Failure
Medicines

Medicines used to relieve symptoms, reduce hospitalizations, improve quality of life, and reduce cardiovascular risk in appropriate patients with heart failure.

What are heart failure medicines?

Heart failure medicines are used to improve symptoms, reduce fluid congestion, slow disease progression, and reduce the risk of hospitalization and death in appropriate patients.

Treatment depends on the type of heart failure, ejection fraction, symptoms, blood pressure, kidney function, potassium level, heart rhythm, and other medical conditions.

Important:

Heart failure treatment should be individualized. Not every patient needs every medicine, and doses often need gradual adjustment and monitoring.

1. ARNI — Sacubitril/Valsartan

Sacubitril/valsartan combines a neprilysin inhibitor with an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB).

It is an important treatment option for many patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).

Important adverse effects

  • Low blood pressure
  • High potassium
  • Worsening kidney function
  • Angioedema
Important:

Sacubitril/valsartan should not be taken together with an ACE inhibitor. A washout period is required when switching from an ACE inhibitor because of angioedema risk.

2. ACE Inhibitors

ACE inhibitors reduce the effects of the renin- angiotensin system and are commonly used when an ARNI is not being used or is not appropriate.

Common examples

  • Enalapril
  • Lisinopril
  • Captopril

Important adverse effects

  • Dry cough
  • Low blood pressure
  • High potassium
  • Changes in kidney function
  • Angioedema

3. Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs)

ARBs block the angiotensin II receptor and may be used in selected patients who cannot tolerate an ACE inhibitor.

Common examples

  • Losartan
  • Valsartan
  • Candesartan

ARBs can cause hypotension, hyperkalemia, and changes in kidney function, so appropriate monitoring is important.

4. Evidence-Based Beta-Blockers

Certain beta-blockers improve outcomes in appropriate patients with HFrEF when started and increased carefully after the patient is clinically stable.

Common examples

  • Carvedilol
  • Metoprolol succinate
  • Bisoprolol

Important considerations

  • Start at a low dose.
  • Increase gradually when appropriate.
  • Monitor heart rate and blood pressure.
  • Monitor symptoms of worsening congestion.
Clinical point:

Beta-blockers are generally initiated or up-titrated when the patient is clinically stable, rather than during significant acute congestion.

5. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) can improve outcomes in appropriate patients with HFrEF.

Common examples

  • Spironolactone
  • Eplerenone

Important risks

  • Hyperkalemia
  • Worsening kidney function
  • Gynecomastia with spironolactone

Potassium and kidney function should be monitored appropriately.

6. SGLT2 Inhibitors

SGLT2 inhibitors are now an important part of guideline-directed treatment for many patients with heart failure, including patients with and without diabetes.

Common examples

  • Dapagliflozin
  • Empagliflozin

Important adverse effects

  • Genital fungal infections
  • Volume depletion in susceptible patients
  • Rare diabetic ketoacidosis
Clinical point:

Their heart failure benefits are not limited to patients who have diabetes.

7. Diuretics

Diuretics help relieve congestion by increasing sodium and water excretion. They are particularly important when patients have fluid overload, edema, or pulmonary congestion.

Common examples

  • Furosemide
  • Torsemide
  • Bumetanide

Important adverse effects

  • Low blood pressure
  • Dehydration
  • Electrolyte abnormalities
  • Changes in kidney function

The dose is adjusted according to congestion, response, kidney function, blood pressure, and electrolyte status.

8. Other Commonly Encountered Medicines

Ivabradine

Ivabradine slows the sinus node heart rate and may be considered in selected patients with HFrEF who remain symptomatic despite appropriate treatment and meet specific heart-rate and rhythm criteria.

Digoxin

Digoxin may be used in selected patients, particularly when symptoms persist despite guideline-directed therapy or when rate control is needed in certain patients with atrial fibrillation.

Digoxin safety:

Digoxin has a narrow therapeutic index. Kidney function, electrolytes, drug interactions, and clinical response are important when monitoring treatment.

Important Drug Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, and MRAs can increase potassium.
  • NSAIDs can worsen fluid retention and kidney function in susceptible patients.
  • Multiple medicines that lower blood pressure can increase the risk of hypotension.
  • Digoxin has important interactions with several medicines.

Monitoring

Monitoring is an essential part of safe heart failure treatment.

  • Blood pressure
  • Heart rate
  • Body weight and fluid status
  • Serum potassium
  • Serum creatinine and kidney function
  • Symptoms and exercise tolerance
  • Medication adherence

When to Seek Urgent Help

  • Severe or rapidly worsening shortness of breath
  • Severe chest pain
  • Fainting or severe dizziness
  • New confusion or extreme weakness
  • Rapidly worsening swelling or fluid retention
  • Severe difficulty breathing while lying down
Emergency:

Severe breathing difficulty or chest pain requires urgent medical assessment.

Clinical Pharmacy Focus

Clinical pharmacy care in heart failure includes reviewing guideline-directed therapy, checking doses, monitoring kidney function and electrolytes, assessing adherence, identifying interactions, and helping patients recognize worsening symptoms.

Treatment should be optimized gradually according to the patient's tolerance and clinical condition.

Patient Education

  • Take medicines consistently as prescribed.
  • Do not stop heart failure medicines suddenly without medical advice.
  • Monitor weight when advised.
  • Follow the recommended diet and fluid plan.
  • Attend scheduled laboratory and clinical follow-up.
  • Report worsening swelling or breathing difficulty.
Remember:

Heart failure treatment usually involves several medicines working together. Each medicine has a different role, and monitoring is essential for safe optimization.

Medicine Safety

Heart failure medicines can affect blood pressure, kidney function, and electrolytes. Regular monitoring is important.

Heart Health

Appropriate medicines, lifestyle measures, monitoring, and adherence all contribute to effective heart failure management.
